§ 107.29(a) opens "Except as provided in paragraph (d) of this section" and (d) had no entry in either direction (#28). It is an entry rather than an absence, and scope: in rather than out, because it is not spent. Both sentences are in force at this map's 2026-01-01 baseline: the first is a standing prohibition with no sunset — after May 17, 2021 no person may operate at night under a waiver issued before April 21, 2021 — and the second records a termination that has occurred. A rule whose condition can no longer be met is not a rule that is no longer in force, and method.md Phase 1 already draws that line: dates inside a rule are ordinary operations over a date the caller supplies, not corpus versioning. The temporal trial is the standing argument that such dates are answerable. The first sentence is night-waiver-bar since the blind second mapping (blind-mapping/ (0014)): it turns on "at night", which this sentence does not, so only that one declines with night-operation. Cases here: a waiver issued before March 16, 2021, one issued between March 16 and April 21, 2021 (not terminated by this sentence), and the termination date itself. The map's earlier record of this carve-out, in night-operation's unmapped reason, said the waivers issued before March 16, 2021 "terminated on that date"; the text says they terminate on May 17, 2021 and March 16, 2021 is the issuance cut-off. Corrected here.
